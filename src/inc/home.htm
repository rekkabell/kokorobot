2026-09-04 Longing for the Morrow

I spent August walking in the rain, and swimming. As I walked my clothes and shoes soaked up water from nearby ferns, I would return home heavy with fern-water. The walk would have me cross a dry river bed populated with hundreds of tiny frogs, I kept my eyes on the ground, careful to avoid them. The day after a big rain, water would start to fill the river, rock-skipping did not always guarantee dry feet. When the sun came out, I would swim, although I did swim once under the rain and seeing the rain drops disturb the water around me was delightful in a Totoro-with-an-umbrella sort-of-way. I brought my swimming goggles and would swim on the surface, looking down below at an eerie scene, at all of the sunken algae-covered trees poking out of the darkness. For the first time in a long while I spent a lot of time playing in the water, doing backflips, trying to do hand-stands in shallow waters. I don't want to be the kind of adult that only lays on the surface of the water, or that comes to the water's edge only to sunbathe. I want to swim, dive, play. Adult Rek will do these things.
In late August we moved the boat somewhere with better internet. Visits from friends had me erupt from my shell, it was nice. Then we began our sail back south. The wind was often blowing out of the southeast, slowing our progress, the short days of northerly wind had us make very long leaps. We sailed 38 NM from Smuggler's Cove to Clam Bay in the Gulf Islands on very light winds. We progressed at a slow 2.5-3 knots all day, we had to go slow anyway to ensure that we would arrive at Porlier Pass at slack tide(around 19h00 that day). A tug and barge kept us company for most of the passage, they too steamed onwards towards that same pass slowly. Having the same boat within sight for a long while on a long sail is nice, like walking with a friend.

READING. I enjoyed Katherine Addison's the Goblin Emperor, but I don't think I'll be reading the other books from this same series, mainly because I'm not super fond of feudalism in books. The Goblin Emperor has a lot of redeeming qualities, Maya as king is just and good and keeps on being just and good throughout the book, although I do tire of seeing women put into forced marriages and being kept from learning, especially from a fantasy book that could have easily moved away from these medieval ideas. It was an enjoyable read, though.
I am continuing to read Flaubert's Madame Bovary, it reminds me a lot of George Elliot's Middlemarch, in that both authors are able to describe the thought process of characters in a way that feels incredibly authentic. Madame Bovary is plagued with ennui, nothing satisfies her, she waits for something incredible to happen, she longs for an ideal that does not exist and is making herself and others around her miserable. It is sad to read, because a lot of people today too suffer from this and seek escape through activities that only offer short-term happiness. It is very human, very relatable, and very well written. I look forward to reading about the court case against Flaubert at the end, which talks about how people attacked him because they thought his book was too obscene, that it would give women bad ideas when in reality it seems to be doing the exact opposite.

She wanted to die, but she also wanted to live in Paris. Gustave Flaubert, Madame Bovary
