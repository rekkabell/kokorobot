2026-10-08 cryptology

For the past month I've been buried in cryptology. Rarely can a thing grab and keep my attention like this. All my tabs were about hand ciphers, one-time-pads, etc. I've been drawing a lot of tables my hand, requiring a kind of precision that really tickled my brain. By the end of September though, with the days getting shorter, I found it harder and harder to document my findings on Rabbit Waves. By then I had done all of the research and had written out all of the pages by hand on paper, but did not have enough power aboard(from solar) to scan and process all of the content. Thankfully, a friend allowed us to use their slip(equipped with luxurious shorepower) for a few days which permitted me to finish the work. The timing was good because by then we had also run out of propane, being at a dock meant we could use our induction plate.
Then, a few days later, we sailed south back towards our winter moorage spot. On the way, we ran into friends aboard their wooden boats, also heading to town, and they shot amazing footage of Pino(Mastodon).

WEBSITE. I wrote a page about processing analog artwork, detailing how I digitize my scanned art.

COOKING. I am very proud that over time I've been able to ween myself off of ingredients that I used to rely on, like nutritional yeast and gluten flour. Nutritional yeast and gluten flour both serve a similar purpose in plant-based meals, they simulate either flavor and/or texture of non-plant-based ingredients. Overall, I do not like to rely on ingredients that are expensive(they both are), hard to find, and that most people are not familiar with. To replace nutritional yeast, say I am preparing a recipe with a "cheesy" taste, I make a mixture of tahini(sesame paste), miso, dijon mustard, garlic powder and apple cider vinegar instead(it is possible to omit the miso). I use this for when I make mac and "cheese" and pizza and am really content with the result. I used gluten flour to make seitan but I've since been grinding rice, lentils, and chickpeas into flour in my grain mill and cooking them together(with a liquid and spices) to make a sort of sliceable savoury loaf. The texture isn't as light as with seitan, but it is very tasty, it has a better nutritional profile and the base ingredients are inexpensive and easy to source.

ART. I finished a page about One-Time Pads for rabbit waves. This was one of the harder pages to assemble, as I had to test a lot of my own ciphers to make sure they work. I also had to do a lot of rewrites to make the text as easy to understand as possible.
I am not the most organized person, I’m trying to change that. I spent a morning making a little custom folder to store paper project files(got one for rabbit waves, another for hakum). I also made templates for both comic pages and for writing text(to have straight and well-spaced text for the RW knowledge pages). The idea with this is that I want to make as little modifications digitally as possible, so the nicer I can get the original to be, the less time i spend on the computer(I have relied on digital editing too much in the past). I realized I have an infinity of patience to do things on paper, but not digitally.
This month I am participating in Drawtober, see them as I make them here.

READING. Last fall I picked up Harper Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird in a street library, I started to read it early last month and finished it very, very quickly because it is a very compelling read. I encouraged Devine to read it too so we could talk about it, and they did(finishing it fast fast). After reading it, I thirsted for integrity and accountability in the people that govern countries, that operate businesses anywhere... in every one, really.
I am re-reading Bram Stoker's Dracula aloud to Devine again, it is rare for me to re-read books, but I am enjoying it(my Renfield is really just a shitty impression of Peter MacNicol's Renfield in Dracula: Dead and Loving it).

MOVIES. I've been on a Sean Baker movie-watching streak. This month I watched both Anora and The Florida Project, and loved both films. The characters feel so genuine, I was shocked at how good the child actors were in Florida Project(i was crying at the end). We secured tickets to see Odyssey at the Imax at the end of the month, I am looking forward to it (Devine has been listening to the music for a few weeks now). I would have liked to go see a special showing of A Simple Machine, but I am trying to limit outings per month(we're on a spending budget).
